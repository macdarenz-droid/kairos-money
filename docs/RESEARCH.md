# Research catalogue — Session 3

Reviewed 13 September 2026. These publications motivate design mechanisms, not a validated Kairos assessment. Descriptive signals and classification thresholds are versioned app heuristics. A transaction cannot establish intent, happiness, pain, awareness or a diagnosis. Projected dollars are arithmetic scenarios, never measured causal effect sizes. Abstracts/author summaries support the bounded summaries below; no claim of systematic review or replication across the target population is made.

## mental-accounting
Thaler (1999), Mental accounting matters, Journal of Behavioral Decision Making, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1099-0771(199909)12:3%3C183::AID-BDM318%3E3.0.CO;2-F). People label and group financial resources and evaluate them within accounts. Kairos uses named goals and sinking funds to make planned allocations visible. These are earmarks, not extra money; funding never changes the ledger or doubles available balances. The paper does not validate the app's recommended allocation.

## pain-of-paying
Prelec & Loewenstein (1998), The Red and the Black: Mental Accounting of Savings and Debt, Marketing Science, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.17.1.4). Their model links consumption and payment experiences. Kairos can describe explicitly recorded instrument shares and offer a payment-check pause. Instrument mix is only a proxy, not a measurement of discomfort. A checking account is not proof of debit-card use; missing instrument metadata prevents this signal.

## tightwad-spendthrift
Rick, Cryder & Loewenstein (2008), Tightwads and Spendthrifts, Journal of Consumer Research, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/523285). The research examines differences between desired spending and experienced payment discomfort. Kairos's Clencher requires explicit self-reported low enjoyment, never a low-spend inference alone. Drifter is a descriptive low-friction/small-purchase pattern, not a diagnosis or the published scale. No questionnaire is reproduced or claimed validated.

## present-bias
Laibson (1997), Golden Eggs and Hyperbolic Discounting, Quarterly Journal of Economics, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355397555253). Time-inconsistent preferences motivate commitment devices in the model. Kairos reports observed payday timing and offers a user-chosen allocation at next pay. Payday clustering is not proof of present bias: bills and purchase scheduling can explain it. No causal saving multiplier is used.

## loss-framing
Kahneman & Tversky (1979), Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk, Econometrica, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185). Choices can depend on reference points and the framing of gains and losses. Kairos describes a buffer as money reserved for essentials. It avoids loss threats, artificial urgency and claims that framing guarantees better choices.

## future-self
Hershfield et al. (2011), Increasing Saving Behavior Through Age-Progressed Renderings of the Future Self, Journal of Marketing Research, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.48.SPL.S23). Experiments examine future-self representations and saving choices. Kairos names the goal, date and intended future use to make a plan concrete. A text goal is not the studied intervention; its dollar outcome is an allocation scenario, not a transferred experimental effect.

## save-more-tomorrow
Thaler & Benartzi (2004), Save More Tomorrow: Using Behavioral Economics to Increase Employee Saving, Journal of Political Economy, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/380085). A voluntary programme links future contribution increases to pay rises. Kairos detects a sustained increase in comparable payslips and proposes earmarking a chosen fraction of the increment. Variable hours and bonuses may mimic a rise; the user confirms the assumption. No automatic transfer or retirement advice is performed.

## implementation-intentions
Gollwitzer (1999), Implementation Intentions: Strong Effects of Simple Plans, American Psychologist, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.54.7.493); Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1). If-then plans connect a specific trigger and response. Every Kairos insight supplies one such action connected to observed evidence. The projected amount is conditional on the user's chosen reduction, not a research-derived saving rate.

## fresh-start
Dai, Milkman & Riis (2014), The Fresh Start Effect: Temporal Landmarks Motivate Aspirational Behavior, Management Science, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901). Temporal landmarks can coincide with aspirational action. Kairos uses month/pay-cycle boundaries as optional plan-review triggers. It does not reset progress, punish missed dates or manufacture a countdown.

## scarcity
Mani, Mullainathan, Shafir & Zhao (2013), Poverty Impedes Cognitive Function, Science, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1238041), with published comment [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1246680) and response [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1246799). Studies examine financial concerns and cognitive load. Kairos reduces decision load when the observed buffer is below five days or explicit debt/fee evidence is concerning. These are precautionary product thresholds, not clinical assessments. Free Australian support: [National Debt Helpline](https://ndh.org.au/), 1800 007 007; checked against [Financial Counselling Australia](https://www.financialcounsellingaustralia.org.au/).

## money-scripts
Klontz, Britt, Mentzer & Klontz (2011), Money Beliefs and Financial Behaviors: Development of the Klontz Money Script Inventory, Journal of Financial Therapy, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4148/jft.v2i1.451). This self-report work concerns money beliefs; bank records do not expose those beliefs. The optional 12-item feature remains disabled: an invented shortened scale would not be the validated instrument. No inferred money-script claim ships.

## east-com-b
[Behavioural Insights Team, EAST](https://www.bi.team/publications/east-four-simple-ways-to-apply-behavioural-insights/), updated 2024; Michie, van Stralen & West (2011), The behaviour change wheel, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42). EAST organizes practical intervention design; COM-B considers capability, opportunity and motivation. Kairos's review checklist requires a feasible small action, a real trigger, transparent assumptions and user control. Social comparison is intentionally not used; neither framework requires fabricating norms.

## experiential-spend
Dunn, Gilbert & Wilson (2011), If money doesn't make you happy, then you probably aren't spending it right, Journal of Consumer Psychology, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2011.02.002). This review discusses spending choices and happiness, including experiences. Kairos may invite a voluntary reflection; it never assigns enjoyment from merchant/category data, recommends spending in distress, or claims a universal experience advantage for a person.

## Claim audit contract
All insight mechanisms reference these stable headings. Measurement statements explain observed inputs rather than psychology. Archetypes explicitly say descriptive pattern and covered days; unavailable axes stay unknown. Forecasts and goal allocations label assumptions and link evidence. The release audit enumerates shipped claim IDs and rejects a missing research mapping. Thresholds and weights are documented in ADR 0008, not attributed to these papers.
