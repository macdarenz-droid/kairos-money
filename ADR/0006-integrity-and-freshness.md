# Integrity and honest freshness

Tier A applies to statements with explicit opening and closing balances, reconciled with exact bigint arithmetic. Tier B checks every running-balance transition in a monotonic file, accepting ascending or descending order. The first row has no predecessor and cannot establish an opening balance. A one-row balance export cannot prove a transition and stays Tier C. An invalid available balance check is quarantined, never downgraded to bypass it.

Tier C has no authoritative balances. Its provided date range is compared with committed account coverage and marked first import, contiguous/overlapping, or a gap. It is usable but visibly balance-unverified. Data health weights its completeness score by 50% confidence (Tier B 90%, Tier A 100%). These are deterministic product weights, not statistical probabilities. Gaps are explicit and excluded from daily averages. PDF corroboration preserves its own statement balance using original contributions rather than overwriting that evidence with an export's later settlement amount.

Update accounts shows the last covered date per account and exports from that date minus six days through today: seven deliberately repeated covered dates. First imports suggest the previous 30 days. Today is muted when all known accounts are stale (the newest exceeds seven days), and still shows each account's individual age. Pending debits are exposed as commitments and excluded from historical income/spending; no safe-to-spend forecast is invented before Session 3.

A chosen weekday reminder is off by default and stored in encrypted app settings. The native inexact alarm holds only the next trigger, and the notification includes no amounts or account names. Import/rollback refreshes scheduling; fresh data postpones the reminder until a chosen weekday that would be stale. Android permission is requested only when enabling it. Android may delay alarms, and opening the app restores scheduling after a reboot. Email ingestion remains disabled.
